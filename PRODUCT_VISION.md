# Product vision

## Working premise

N//XT should help a person understand how their own body, mind, behavior, spiritual life, and
environment relate over time. Logging is the input; understanding is the product. Physical and
mental health have architectural parity from the beginning.

The product starts with population science to initialize estimates, then progressively weights observed longitudinal data more heavily. It must always separate measured, entered, imported, estimated, modeled, and AI-estimated values.

## Who it serves

It is a general-purpose platform, not a single-person tracker and not a weight-loss-only product. A
person may want to understand mood or anxiety patterns, manage stress, improve sleep, track
symptoms or medication context, improve nutrition, become more active, change weight, or simply
understand their data. Optional modules should stay out of the way when they are irrelevant.

The product must not diagnose mental illness from self-reported signals. Its intelligence layer can
surface observations with uncertainty, such as “Your reported mood has been lower during weeks when
your sleep has also been shorter,” but not “You are depressed.”

## Opening premise

The opening should make the product legible to many kinds of people without sorting them into
separate categories: `BODY // MIND // LIFE` and “See the whole you.” Health is more than any one
number; it includes movement, food, sleep, feelings, medication, habits, history, and the ways those
things affect one another. The product is here to help someone discover patterns, understand what
affects them, and decide what comes next.

The point is not to make every experience look positive. It is to give the whole person equal
attention. Weight is information about someone, not their identity. A diagnosis, medication, bad
week, or personal best is information too—not the whole person.

Identity language must remain precise and inclusive. Sex assigned at birth, gender identity, and
pronouns are separate concepts. Sex assigned at birth may provide a default technical population
reference for an equation, but it is never a statement of gender identity. None of these fields is
required to access the product, and no user should have to identify as mentally ill—or disclose an
identity—to access supportive health tools.

## Product principles

1. Show the useful answer first, with a path to the evidence.
2. Prefer trends over noisy single measurements.
3. Never turn estimates into facts through visual confidence or false precision.
4. Keep safety, privacy, consent, and data ownership visible.
5. Begin useful with minimal data and become more personal with consistency.
6. Make the architecture ready for web, PWA, Android, providers, and future sync without prematurely building a distributed system.
7. Health is physical and mental; mind is not a bolt-on feature.
8. Nothing about a person is a failed streak. Missing data is context, not a moral failure.
9. Be curious, never judgmental; notice patterns without inventing causation.
10. Support people and connection to appropriate help; do not replace qualified care.
11. The user owns their story and their data, including what AI and cross-domain analysis may access.
12. Continue from where you are. //

## Phase 1 promise

A new user can create a profile, choose multiple areas of interest across body and mind, enter
weight, see transparent starting estimates, log food and activity, understand a trend, and keep
using the app offline. The first-run opening is a brief, skippable two-page introduction before
setup begins. Everything in this slice is local to the browser by default.

The next vertical slices will add private, non-scored wellbeing observations and optional life-event
context. These records are designed for self-understanding and future longitudinal context, not
clinical conclusions.

## Temporary tone

Do not sound like a wellness company. Sound like someone who understands why the product needs to
exist. Copy should be emotionally honest, warm without being saccharine, hopeful without becoming
inspirational-poster language, direct, intelligent, and nonjudgmental. It should be comfortable
acknowledging that life can be difficult.

Avoid generic phrases such as “your wellness journey,” “be your best self,” “a healthier you,”
“unlock your potential,” “find your balance,” “a calmer way,” and “live your healthiest life.” Some
people are not trying to become their best self. Sometimes they are just trying to keep going. And
that counts.

Kintsugi is a philosophical reference, not decorative shorthand for trauma. The product can say
that damage does not erase value, struggling does not make someone a failure, mental health is health,
history does not have to be hidden, and a setback does not erase progress. It must not imply that
suffering is beautiful, necessary, deserved, or guaranteed to make someone stronger.
